### 0.4 The v4 identity constraint — the seat axis is inert at `seats = 6`

V3-PLAN §0.4 admits a new mechanism in one of three ways: (a) a new axis inert at legacy
settings, (b) a new artifact, (c) a deliberate re-freeze. v4 uses (a) for the axis and (b) for
the ring artifact, and **forbids (c)**. Concretely:

- At `seats = 6`, every painted tier, every score, every disabled reason, every Method-view line
  and every byte of `data/model.json` outside the new `constants.ladder` block is identical to
  `1d988f5` — the ring is a **separate artifact**, `data/ring.json`, not a `model.json` sub-block
  (§2.4). Proven three ways: the three legacy fixtures green with an empty `--check` diff; gate
  I48's tripwire (zero `data/ring.json` on a copy → the seats-6 surface does not move); and D11's
  dual determinism holding across the rebuild.
- The 6-max ladder is **not renamed**: `UTG · HJ · CO · BTN · SB · BB` remain the keys of every
  legacy fixture row, URL state and gate id. The nine-seat ladder gives the same structural seat
  the name `LJ` (§2.1), and gate I50 is where that equivalence becomes a claim that can fail.

## 2. The design, decided

### 2.1 The ladder — one array, two views, no new literals

`policy.mjs` gets one canonical constant and one derivation:

```
LADDER9   = ['UTG','UTG1','UTG2','LJ','HJ','CO','BTN','SB','BB']     // keys; display 'UTG+1', 'UTG+2'
seatsFor(6) = ['UTG','HJ','CO','BTN','SB','BB']                        // the legacy list, verbatim
seatsFor(9) = LADDER9
```

`POSITIONS` stays exported and **equal to `seatsFor(6)`** so the 15 importers and every fixture
row are untouched. The structural fact the two views share: a seat is defined by **how many
non-blind seats and how many blind seats act behind it**, and 6-max `UTG` (3 behind, 2 blinds) is
structurally 9-max `LJ`. The name `LJ` is chosen (over `MP`) because it makes that identity
legible on the rail: the six-seat table is the nine-seat table with its first three seats empty.

Rule (V3-PLAN §0.2, now enforced by gate I51(c)): **no new seat-name literal anywhere** — not in
policy, gates, tests, the shell, or docs prose that the gates read. Every consumer takes the list
from `seatsFor(seats)` or a structural function of it. The five re-typed `NEST_CHAIN` literals
and `baseline.mjs`'s two six-seat arrays are replaced by imports in the same stage.

literal's seat set at 6 *and* that it names the right seats at 9. Both halves are asserted by

stays, is recorded as an offender in METHODOLOGY, and I51(b) records the exception — it is not
laundered into a predicate that happens to match.

### 2.3 The early-seat constants — derived by rule, not typed

`baseRaise` and `baseR` need values for `UTG`, `UTG1`, `UTG2` at nine seats. **Nobody types a
number.** Each is derived from a named rule whose anchor already exists in the model, the rule is
itself a constant in `constants.ladder`, and the derived values are printed in the Method view
beside the rule that produced them.

`baseRaise` — the opening ladder. The model already owns one seat step: `straddle.seat = 0.77 =
1/√(1.250 · 1.350)` (`policy.mjs:367–372`), the geometric mean of the two early steps of the
ladder, explicitly excluding the CO→BTN step. v4 names that number `ladder.earlyStep`. It stays
**two constants with equal values, one anchor** — `straddle.seat` keeps its own value and gains
`derivedFrom: 'ladder.earlyStep'`; it does **not** become a live reference. A live reference would
couple I26, whose whole job is to perturb `straddle.seat` (that is how the "BTN keeps its 0.45"
claim was falsified — the `seatPinned` comment at `policy.mjs:373–378`), to the opening ladder:
a perturbation above 1 would invert the nine-seat `baseRaise` ladder that I51(a) requires to be
strictly increasing, and the two gates would fight. Instead I51(a) asserts the equality
`straddle.seat === ladder.earlyStep` on the **shipped** constants, so they cannot drift, and I26's
perturbation is exempt from it by name and does not flow into the ladder. Note also that
`straddle.seat` is a **width** factor, not a realization factor: `seatWidthFactor` (`:749–753`)
returns it for every seat and multiplies `baseRaise[pos]` in `widthFor` (`:1394`). The rule:
`baseRaise(UTG2) = baseRaise(LJ) · earlyStep`, `UTG1 = LJ · earlyStep²`, `UTG = LJ · earlyStep³`.
What the rule yields — **recorded here as the rule's output, not as chosen numbers**: with
`baseRaise(LJ) = 0.16` (the legacy UTG value, unchanged), `0.1232 · 0.0949 · 0.0731`. Whether a
7.3 % nine-handed UTG open is defensible is exactly the question the red team is asked (§7.4);
the plan's position is that a geometric ladder is the only anchor the model has, and an
unanchored "looks right" override is what §0.3 forbids.

`baseR` — the per-seat realization factor (`0.97 · 0.99 · 1.02 · 1.06 · 0.90 · 0.93`,
`baseRealization(pos, d) = baseR[pos]^(1 + beta·u(d))` at `policy.mjs:873`, consumed by
`realization` at `:879`). Its seat-dependence is small and non-monotone, and the model records no
measurement that distinguishes one out-of-position early seat from another. It is **not**
scoring-only: `baseR` also moves painted *width* at non-reference depth through
`depthWidthFactor`, which is the ratio of two `baseRealization` calls (`policy.mjs:634`) applied
as `g` in `widthFor` (`:1389`, `:1398`). The v3-default fixture's 12 lanes include d = 40 and
d = 250, so R1's flat-vs-step choice moves painted width in **8 of the 12 lanes** — for the new
seats only — and is inert only at d = 100, where `g === 1`. I51 must read both surfaces.
**Two candidate rules, decided by
measurement, not by preference** (§3 rule R1): (flat) the three new seats inherit `baseR(LJ)`;
(step) they continue the UTG→HJ step downward (`0.95 · 0.93 · 0.91`). Decision rule: run I51's
nesting and monotonicity checks under both; if only one passes, it ships; if both pass, **flat**
ships (fewer new numbers, and no claim the model cannot back), and the step rule is recorded as
the rejected alternative with its readings. Either way `constants.ladder.baseRRule` names which.

Both rules are **opinion**. They ship `kind: 'estimate'`, badged in the Method view, bounded by
I51, and the red team's memo on each is committed to `docs/refutations/V4.md`.

### 2.4 N_eff above seven — the ring artifact

At nine seats `N_eff` exceeds 7 routinely: rfi from UTG at `v = 0.90` gives `raw ≈ 5.54`, and the
limps node reaches `raw ≈ 8.24` at **UTG+1** over three limpers — UTG itself is structurally
disabled at that node (§2.2), so the 9-max UTG limps reading is not a legal setting. Over **four**
limpers, which is UI-reachable and is where 6-max already peaks (V3-BRIEF :211: max raw 8.23 at
limps/HJ, VPIP 90, 4 limpers, straddle), UTG+1 reads **9.14**, and **9.96** straddled — so
`nMax(9) = 9` does not remove the clamp, it moves it, and R3's census measures the residue.
Clamping at 7 would badge a large share of the new surface `extrapolated`; regenerating
`model.json` at `NMAX = 9` would add **~7.0 KB** — measured, not estimated: `eq[]` is 4,242 B over
861 numbers and `vDelta[]` 20,833 B over 4,305 numbers, i.e. 4.9 B per number over the **123
non-empty cells** (22 of the 145 keys are structurally empty, METHODOLOGY §2.3), so two more N
columns is `123 × 12 × 4.9 ≈ 7,164 B` — taking `core` from 116.0 K to ~123 K past its 120 KB
ceiling and re-measuring the frozen layer. Neither is acceptable, so:

**`data/ring.json`** — a new artifact in the P3 idiom (`checkdown-matrix.json`, `equilibrium.json`):

- Generated by **`scripts/generate-ring.mjs`** (new; library in `scripts/lib/ring.mjs`), which
  deals nine villains per trial with the same `TRIALS` and `se` regime as `generate-data.mjs`
  (`meta.trials.cell = 100000`, `se.cell = 0.16`; lattice likewise) under **two named seeds**
  `rundown-v4/ring-A` / `-B`, and writes per cell `eq[7..8]` and `vDelta[v][7..8]` (0-based:
  the `N = 8, 9` columns) plus `meta { generatorHash, contentHash, seeds, trials, se }`.
- The prefix technique yields `N = 1..7` from the same deals for free. Those columns are **not
  shipped**; they are the artifact's falsifier: D12(c) asserts they agree with the shipped
  `cells[*].eq[0..6]` within `2 · se.cell` on every cell — an independent, differently-seeded
  reproduction of the v1 measurement layer, obtained at no extra cost.
- `--check` re-runs the generator and refuses to write on any byte difference (D12(a)), exactly
  as the matrix and equilibrium generators do.
- Wall time is **its own budget**, pre-registered at **300 s** — derived, not chosen: the shipped
  pipeline's two `NMAX`-scaling stages are S2 (12 s) + S2L (101 s) = 113 s at seven villains
  (METHODOLOGY :1955), cost scales ≈ linearly in villains because the per-trial draw is
  `NEED = 5 + NMAX * 4` (`scripts/lib/mc.mjs:106`), and the ring runs **two** seeds:
  `2 × 113 × 9/7 ≈ 291 s`, rounded up to 300. It is measured at stage S2 and written into
  METHODOLOGY. It is **not** added to `generate-data.mjs`'s 188 s.

> **Amended by the owner (2026-09-08, after run 1 — stage S2, lane R).** The derivation above is
> **falsified by measurement** and is kept as written. Linear-in-villains holds for `runMulti`
> (per-cell 19 → 22 ms, **1.16×**) and fails for `runMultiFiltered` (per-cell lattice
> Σ 189 → 1,170 ms, **6.2×**): by the ninth villain 40 of 52 cards are dead, the VPIP-filtered
> pools exhaust rejection sampling, and each exhaustion burns the full `RANGE_TRIES = 4,000`
> before falling back to a random hand (v = 25 fallback rate 0.029 % → 5.744 %). End to end the
> ring's two lattice stages ran 584.1 s and 599.1 s against the shipped S2L's 101 s (5.9×,
> reproducing the per-cell ratio within 5 %); the two-seed wall was **1,213.6 s** at four workers.
> R2's halving would give ≈ 622 s and eight workers gave 660.8 s, so neither meets 300 s and R2's
> blocker clause fired as written. The budget is therefore **re-derived from the measured cost
> model, stage by stage, in the same shape as the derivation it replaces**:
> `2 × (12 × 1.16 + 101 × 6.2) ≈ 1,280 s`, pre-registered at **1,280 s**, measured at four
> workers — the derivation's own regime; METHODOLOGY records that worker count cannot move a
> number, so a larger count is a wall-clock trade, never a way to meet the budget. The lattice
> ships at the full `generate-data` regime: no halving, no seed dropped. It remains a one-off cost
> per change to the construction, never per model run or per verify, exactly as the checkdown
> matrix's 21 s is. Run 1's evidence: `docs/spikes/V4-ring.md` §3.2 and §7 (lane R, branch
> `worktree-wf_3c958a35-f26-4`). The finding beneath the number — 5.7 % of the ninth villain's
> v = 25 draws are not from the range at all — is a measurement-layer limitation METHODOLOGY
> carries beside limitation 20, not something the budget launders.
- The build embeds it **as its own injected payload**, exactly on the `data/equilibrium.json`
  precedent (the full variant's `eq 73K` region, printed by the build census as its own region):
  a top-level `ring: N * 1024` budget row in **both** `VARIANTS.lite.budgets` and

  **D12(d)** (§2.7). It is **not** a `model.json` sub-budget: `model.json` grows only by
  `constants.ladder` (a few hundred bytes, absorbed by `core`'s existing headroom), so D6's `BUD`,
  `CORE_BUDGET` and `core` at `scripts/gates/data.mjs:584–592` need **no edit** and `core`
  116.0 / 120 KB is genuinely not raised. The page-side markup is the separate `@block:ring`

## 7. Orchestration — one launch, flat context

### 7.1 The contract with the launching session

The launching session is **Fable, top-level, and does three things only**: launches the
workflow, receives its return value, and reports it. It never reads a source file, a worker's
detail file, a diff or a log. Everything below is enforced by the script, not by discipline:

- **One `Workflow` call.** `.claude/workflows/v4.js` runs S0–S6 end to end; control flow is
  deterministic JS outside the session; the session sees the launch line and one return.
- **Every `agent()` returns a schema.** Reports are structured and capped (§7.3). Anything longer
  is a **file** — `docs/spikes/V4-*.md`, `docs/refutations/V4.md`, or the run's scratchpad — and
  the return carries the path. The final return prints as **one line** (~460–900 characters) and
  **51–55 lines pretty-printed** — measured against the script, not asserted — every line of it a
  number, a boolean or a short path (§7.3).
- **`blockers` is reserved** (v3 HOUSE rule): only what must stop the commit. Findings, resolved
  trade-offs and deferred items go in `summary`.
- **The script's own Fable tier is the last Fable layer** (V3-PLAN §11): the run is launched from
  a top-level session, never from inside a sub-orchestrator; the milestone architect and the
  triage step are read-only.
- **No questions to the user mid-run.** A genuine decision only the owner can make is a blocker
  in the return; the owner adjudicates in prose; the relaunch uses `resumeFromRunId`, which
  replays completed `agent()` calls from cache so only the changed stage re-runs (the P3
  precedent, decision 13).
- **Model tiering, every call explicit** (v3 policy): `fable @ high` architect (work-orders for
  the max-effort steps, triage before the fix round; read-only); `opus @ xhigh` default worker
  (lanes, integration, refuters, docs); `opus @ max` for the ladder generalisation (S1), the
  fixture freeze + identity proof (S3) and the fix round; `sonnet @ medium` for the precheck and
  the verify agents; `haiku` for the commit.

**Contention registry.** `policy.mjs`: S1 only; frozen for S2 — a lane that needs a policy
change *files it* in its return (`policyDeltas`), and S3 applies the deltas serially, then re-runs
I48 before continuing. `src/shell.html`: S1 touches its three named ranges (its §7.2 row), R its
three named sites (the lane-R row), U owns the rest; the merge is line-disjoint by construction. `scripts/gates/ring.mjs` (F) and `ring-artifact.mjs` (R)
are separate files so the two lanes never share one. `scripts/gates/index.mjs`: **lane F is the
single writer** of `EXPECTED_IDS` and of `gates/reserved.mjs`, and it registers all seven ids
(I48–I52, D12, D13) in one place — R reports `D12` in its return and F appends it. This is not a
shared append-only file: a mismatch between the declared sequence and that frozen literal makes the
runner *throw* rather than fail a gate (§5.2), so two lanes each appending is a failure mode with no
gate to catch it, and S3 has nothing to reconcile. `freeze-tiers.mjs`: F writes the kind, S3
runs it — a stop-the-world event, once. `variant.mjs` / `build.mjs`: S3 only.

**The S1→S2 hand-off.** S1's work stays uncommitted (above), but a worktree is cut from `HEAD` and
does not carry the main tree's uncommitted work — measured in the v3 P2 run — while all four lanes
need `seatsFor` and the structural functions to *exist*. So S1's last action is a snapshot ceremony
that changes nothing about its tree: `git add -A` (stage, do not commit), `SNAP=$(git stash create)`
(a commit **object** for the working tree — it touches neither HEAD, nor the index, nor the stash
list), `git branch v4-s1-base $SNAP`. Each lane's first action is to grep its own `policy.mjs` for
`seatsFor` and, if it is absent, `git merge --ff-only v4-s1-base` — a no-op in the world where the
harness does carry dirty state into worktrees, and a blocker (never a guess) if the branch is
missing. S3 folds the snapshot back before merging: if the tree is dirty and `git diff v4-s1-base`
is empty, `git reset --soft v4-s1-base` + `git commit --amend`, so the lane merges apply to a clean
tree that already shares their base; if that diff is **not** empty, S3 stops, because the snapshot
no longer describes the tree and a merge would silently drop work. That mid-run commit is merge
bookkeeping: **the boundary commit is still S6's and still single**, and nothing is pushed.

### 7.3 Schemas and caps

Every schema has `summary` (≤ 12 lines), `blockers` (array, reserved), and stage-specific
measured fields as **numbers**, never prose. The final return:

```
{ green: bool, committed: sha|null, gates: {total, fail:[]}, tests: {total, fail},
  build: {lite:{total, app, appCore, ring}, full:{total, app, appCore, ring}}, fixture: {settings, path},
  identity: {i48: bool, i50: {rfiExact: bool, clauses: n}}, ring: {wallSec, prefixAgreement},
  raises: [{ceiling, from, to, shrinkFirstBytes}], redTeam: {constants, unanchored},
  detailFiles: [paths], blockers: [] }
```

That object is the whole of what enters the launching session: 1 line compact, 51–55 pretty-printed.
Every variable-length part is **hard-capped in the script** so that a red run cannot print larger
than a green one — `gates.fail` ≤ 4, `raises` ≤ 3, `redTeam.unanchored` ≤ 3, `detailFiles` ≤ 6,
`blockers` ≤ 4 at 150 characters each, every string whitespace-collapsed and truncated. Shape won
over line count: reaching 40 pretty-printed lines would mean flattening `build` and `identity`, i.e.
renaming fields this section names. The Fable architect's work-orders and triage are written to
`docs/spikes/V4-workorders.md`, not returned.

### 7.4 The red team

Three refuters, each briefed with §3, §4 and `docs/spikes/V4-ladder.md`, each writing one memo
per constant to the scratchpad; a resolver merges them into `docs/refutations/V4.md` and applies
majority rule. Targets, in order of expected heat: `ladder.earlyStep`'s promotion (is one seat of
the *six*-seat ladder the right step for seats that ladder never contained?); the geometric rule's
output at UTG; R1's choice; the ring's two-seed tolerance and the choice of `NMAX = 9` (why not
8); D12(c)'s `2 · se` agreement band; the `extrapolated` census thresholds in R3; every raise in
§2.7 and its shrink sentence; the `LJ` naming (a rail label is a claim too).

### 7.5 Failure handling

Exactly **one fix round** per run (opus max, under Fable triage), then a blocker report. The
report names the stage, the file paths, and the decision the owner must make in one sentence
each. Relaunch with `resumeFromRunId`; unchanged stages replay from cache.

The owner's adjudication reaches the run through **one argument**, not through edited briefs:

```
Workflow({ scriptPath: ".claude/workflows/v4.js", resumeFromRunId: "<id>",
           args: { adjudication: { stage: "S3", text: "<the owner's decision, in prose>" } } })
```

The prose is appended to **that stage's prompt only** (stage ids `S0`, `S1`, `S2-R|U|F|K`, `S3`,
`S4`, `S5`, `S6`), so every earlier `agent()` call keeps a byte-identical prompt and replays from
cache — appending it to every brief would change every prompt and destroy the very cache
`resumeFromRunId` exists for. With no `args`, every prompt is unchanged. The prose **binds** the
stage that receives it and may not weaken a gate, widen a tolerance or authorise `--force`.

### 7.6 Launch

From a top-level session, on `main`, tree clean:

```
Workflow({ scriptPath: ".claude/workflows/v4.js" })
```

The script is written from §7.2–7.5 and `.claude/workflows/v3.js`'s helpers (`HOUSE`, the model
tiers, `closeMilestone`, `redTeam`, the schemas, worktree isolation) and committed beside this
plan. A `/v4` skill mirroring `/v3` is optional sugar; the launch line above is the contract.
