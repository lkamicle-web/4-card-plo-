# P5 red team — the refutation record

Three independent refuters were pointed at every new opinion constant this phase shipped — the
sub-cell top-N's claim that it introduces **no new constant at all**, the per-block byte ceiling
`budgets.blocks.topn`, and the `budgets.app` raise that ceiling forced — with one instruction:
**falsify it**. Perturb the number, delete the flag, replace the anchor's prose with a lie, and
report what the repository does about it. Three tally keys drew **nine memos**. This file is the
record, committed with the phase.

**Why it is a file and not a paragraph.** The house rule is *constants need anchors*: a number
either stands on a measurement, or it ships flagged, gated and badged with the reason it cannot.
That rule is only worth what the audit behind it is worth, and an audit that leaves no trace is a
claim rather than a check. The memos below are reproduced **verbatim**, including the places where
refuters disagree with each other, the places where a memo is wrong about the repository, and every
sentence critical of work this phase had just finished. Nothing was trimmed to look better. All three
`app` memos open by pointing out that the constant they were briefed on is **already superseded**
— the brief says 392 KB, the tree says 398 KB — and all three are printed exactly as written,
because a red team that is quietly re-briefed before publication is a red team nobody can audit.

## The verdict

A constant is **majority-unanchored** when more than half the refuters who attacked it returned
*unanchorable*. **No tally key came back that way this phase**: zero of nine memos returned
unanchorable, and every one of the three keys was returned `[falsifiable]` by all three refuters.
So §6 is asked nothing it has to answer, and this file's whole substance is the second question —
*where does the enforcement fall short of the sentence it enforces?*

| Constant | Memos | Unanchorable | V3-PLAN §6 | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| item 10 — **no new constant**; N is the cell's own `ex.length` | 3 | 0 | nothing to anchor, because there is nothing to name | claim stands, and is exact. **Gate strengthened**: I47(e) now reads the whole of `want` and pins it to the identity, after all three refuters walked a chosen N straight past a one-line digit scan |
| `budgets.blocks.topn` = 5 KB | 3 | 0 | measured + 5% (4,844 B → 5,087 B), rounded up to the whole KB | stands, and it is the best-anchored of the three: bounded downward by the build's own measurement and upward by the cap-sum invariant. The **+5% itself** is prose plus a shared sum — recorded |
| `budgets.app` 388 → 392 KB | 3 | 0 | forced by the cap-sum rule, not by the artifact | stands; **fully determined from below, unenforced from above** — recorded. The briefed figure is already superseded twice inside this phase, at 398 KB |

## What changed because of it

One gate strengthening, two documentation repairs, and a set of recorded findings that changed no
code. The strengthening was **armed** before it was believed: every perturbation that shipped green
in a refuter's memo was re-run against the new clause and made to fail.

### 1. I47(e) — the one clause that was holed, and the repair is armed

The claim under attack is the strongest one item 10 makes: *the list length is read off the cell,
not chosen, so §6 has nothing to anchor*. All three refuters agreed the **behavioural** half is
real — move the effective N off `ex.length` by one and I47 goes red on 123 cells, naming them — and
all three found the same hole in the **syntactic** half, independently:

```js
const wantSrc = block.src.slice(block.src.indexOf('function want('));
const wantBody = wantSrc.slice(0, wantSrc.indexOf('\n'));   // <- the first LINE, not the function
```

`want` ships as a one-liner, so the window looked like the function. Reformat it and it is not.
Both holes were reproduced on this tree, through the family's own `build(ctx)`:

| Perturbation of `want` | I47 before | I47 after |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — a chosen `6` on line 2 of a reformatted three-line body | **PASS** (the hole) | **FAIL** |
| **B** — a digit-free chosen six: `var NSIX = 'aaaaaa'.length;` returned in the read's place | **PASS** (the hole) | **FAIL** |
| **C** — a chosen `5` | FAIL, on the per-cell equality | FAIL, 125 findings, the first naming a cell and its own `ex.length` |
| **D** — refuter 2's launderer: `var NCAP = MS.length ? 6 : 6;` with `Math.min(NCAP, cell.ex.length)` | PASS, per refuter 2 | **FAIL** |
| the shipped block, unmodified | PASS | **PASS** |

**A** was caught only by `test/subcell.test.mjs`, whose window already ran to the closing brace —
so the shipped *gate* was weaker than the test standing behind it, and the sentence in I47's own
detail string was the test's claim rather than the gate's. **B** and **D** were caught by neither:
they are chosen constants that a digit regex cannot see, and the equality beside it is vacuous
because all 123 live cells ship exactly six examples, which makes *read* and *chosen six*
observationally identical everywhere the page renders.

The repair does not widen the regex. It asserts the thing METHODOLOGY actually claims:

- the window is `want`'s **whole body**, brace-matched, so a reformat cannot move code out of view;
- the digit scan stays, because it is the clause that names the failure it was written for (someone
  "fixing" an empty list by typing a 6) in the words that failure deserves;
- and the body, comments stripped and whitespace collapsed, must **be** the identity read —
  `return (cell.ex && cell.ex.length) || 0;`. A cap, a `Math.min`, or a length laundered through a
  string cannot enter `want` from anywhere in the block, whatever it is spelled with.

Attack **B** now fails with one problem, and it names itself:

```
(e) want() is not the identity read `return (cell.ex && cell.ex.length) || 0;`
    — it is `return (cell.ex && cell.ex.length) ? NSIX : 0;`
```

This makes any rewrite of that one line a deliberate re-decision, which is the correct cost for a
function whose entire job is to have no opinion. **No shipped file changed**: the repair is in
`scripts/gates/subcell.mjs`, both artifacts are byte-identical, and `build --check` reports 2/2
current.

### 2. Two documentation repairs

**METHODOLOGY §8 overstated the clause.** It read "I47 clause (e) asserts there is no numeric
literal for it in the block" — which was false in two directions at once: the scan never covered
the block (only one line of one function), and *no numeric literal* was never the property worth
asserting, since attack **B** carries no numeric literal at all. The sentence now says what the
clause now does, and says where the 6 actually comes from (below).

**`test/variant.test.mjs` quoted a superseded measurement next to a live ceiling** — refuter 2's
find. Its calibration-step note read "398 KB is measured + 1.3% against a page at 392.7 KB" while
the manifest and METHODOLOGY §9.11 had both moved to 393.7 KB and +1.1% after the methodology
rewrite spent 1.0 KB of core. Both readings were true when written; only one is true now. The
comment now carries the step's own measurement **and** its supersession, which is the shape the
manifest strings already use and the defect the P3 red team caught in this exact string.

### 3. Recorded, and deliberately not repaired

**N is inherited, not invented — and nothing bounds it.** All three refuters land on the same
place: item 10 truthfully adds **zero new constants**, but the number that decides what the reader
sees is `spanExamples(E.exByAdj[unit], 6)` at `scripts/generate-data.mjs:353` (with `want = 6` as
`spanExamples`'s own default in `taxonomy.mjs`), a pre-existing literal that appears in no
`model.constants` key and is named nowhere in §6. Its only standing defence is a restatement —
`test/taxonomy.test.mjs:145` passes 6 in and asserts 6 comes out, for one cell.

Refuters 2 and 3 both showed nothing bounds it, and this was re-run here with the **full** GREEN
check rather than the data family alone: with every cell's `ex` truncated 6 → 3 in `data/model.json`,
`node scripts/verify.mjs` **exits 0 with all 62 gates passing**, and the two tests that do fail —
`the payload hashes describe the payload` and `D7: model.json as emitted is inside the 220 KB
ceiling, and that IS the file on disk` — fail because the payload was *hand-edited*, not because
its lists got shorter. Both are stamped over the payload's bytes; a model **re-emitted** at N = 3
would restamp them and ship green.

No gate was added for it, deliberately. Bounding N would mean naming 6 — creating in P5's ledger
the very constant item 10 correctly claims not to have, for a choice that predates this phase and
belongs to the Example-hands grid rather than to the top-N. What was owed was honesty about the
inheritance, and METHODOLOGY now carries it: N is the Example-hands convention the sub-cell list
adopts, not a quantity this layer derives.

**The byte budgets are anchored from below and open from above.** Both ceilings are refused
downward by real measurements — `topn` at 4 KB dies on the block's own compiled 4,844 B, `app` at
393 KB dies on the page's own 393.7 KB — and refused upward, one kilobyte at a time, by the
cap-sum invariant in `test/variant.test.mjs`, which survives deleting the `deepEqual` restatement
beside it. Refuter 3 confirmed the P3 filler test is closed for this block: 200 B of filler inside
`@block:topn` is green, 300 B is red, so the live slack is 276 B and the cap bounds the **feature**
rather than its name.

What no gate asserts is that a ceiling is not too **loose**. `app` at 460 KB (refuter 1) and at
512 KB (refuter 3) ship completely green; the only shipped statement about the +5% rule is
`assert.match(VARIANTS.lite.budgetSource, /5%/)`, a regex on prose. Refuter 3 also showed the
per-feature *split* of the saturated 38 KB is unanchored: a kilobyte moved from `gto` to `topn`
leaves everything green with `topn` then sitting at measured +23.9%. Two concrete repairs were
offered — a clause asserting `cap <= ceil(measured * 1.05)` from the build's own block census, and
a test pinning `app` to equality with `appCore + capSum` rather than merely above it. Neither was
taken in this phase: both are new bounding gates on the byte ceremony rather than repairs to a
refuted anchor, no memo returned unanchorable, and the practical exposure is small — `appCore` has
0.7 KB of live headroom, the cap sum is at exact equality, and `total` backstops `app` near 410 KB.
They are recorded here so the next byte raise has them in hand rather than rediscovering them.

**The `app` figure in the tally key is history.** The brief quotes 392 KB against a page at
387.6 KB; the tree ships 398 KB against 393.7 KB (lite) / 393.9 KB (full), moved twice more inside
P5 by the calibration step and the methodology rewrite. All three refuters attacked the **live**
number rather than the briefed one, which is why their memos read against 398. The manifest strings
in `scripts/lib/variant.mjs` recite each step in order and end with the paragraph that supersedes
the readings above it — refuter 1 checked this explicitly and called it "history rather than the
stale-quote defect the P3 red team caught". No change was needed there.
