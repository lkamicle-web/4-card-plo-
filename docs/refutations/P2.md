# P2 red team — the refutation record

Three independent refuters were pointed at every new opinion constant this phase shipped — the
payoff estimator's fit and the CFR+ solver's thresholds — with one instruction: **falsify it**.
Perturb the number, delete the flag, replace the formula string with a lie, and report what the
repository does about it. Eighteen constants (or constant-shaped claims) drew **fifty-four memos**.
This file is the record, committed with the phase.

**Why it is a file and not a paragraph.** The house rule is *constants need anchors*: a number
either stands on a measurement, or it ships flagged, gated and badged with the reason it cannot.
That rule is only worth what the audit behind it is worth, and an audit that leaves no trace is a
claim rather than a check. The memos below are reproduced **verbatim**, including the places where
refuters disagree with each other, the places where a memo is wrong about the repository, and every
sentence critical of work this phase had just finished. Nothing was trimmed to look better.

## The verdict, and what changed because of it

A constant is **majority-unanchored** when more than half the refuters who attacked it returned
*unanchorable*. Three tally entries came back that way, covering **two distinct claims** — the
duplicate is a punctuation split in the tally keys (`ZERO NEW CONSTANTS IN THE POT GEOMETRY` with
and without a trailing period), and both halves are answered together.

For each, the question is the one V3-PLAN §6 asks: does the plan already say it *cannot be anchored*
— in which case its three legs (**gated + flagged in `constants` + labelled and badged in the Method
view**) must all actually hold — or does the plan claim an anchor the red team has now refuted, in
which case the anchor is gone and no replacement may be invented.

**Neither of these two is either of those.** In both cases every refuter who called the row
unanchorable *also verified that the anchor is true*, and said so explicitly: `REF_META.sprs` really
is `[1, 4, 10]`, so 10 really is the largest depth S-B simulated; and `1 + 2*spr` really is the
node's pot plus both stacks, so the geometry really does introduce no constant. What was refuted in
both cases is **the enforcement, not the anchor** — "10 is indistinguishable from `Infinity` to
every shipped claim", "no gate can currently distinguish it from its opposite". That is a third
outcome §6 does not enumerate, and the disposition it calls for is neither an invented number nor a
flag: it is the assertion the prose already implied.

**No blockers.** Neither majority-unanchored claim needed an invented number, and neither could
legitimately take §6's flagged idiom — that idiom requires a `constants` entry, and Grade C forbids
any estimator number from reaching `constants`, `data/model.json` or the Method view, which
`test/payoff-model.test.mjs` asserts as a property of the tree. Nor does either need one: an
omission of the *third* leg (**bounded by a gate**) was the whole defect, and that is what was
repaired. No tolerance was widened anywhere in this pass, and every clause added below was shown to
fail before it was believed.

### Why the pot geometry is not "flagged" instead

It would have been cheaper to write `UNANCHORED.push({ name: 'potMultiplier', badge: 'estimate' })`
and move on. That would have been a lie. A flag says *this number was chosen and we cannot justify
it*; the `2` in `1 + 2*spr*w` is the two stacks behind the two players, and calling it an opinion
would put a badge on arithmetic while leaving the real defect — that nothing could tell it from
`100` — exactly where it was. The same goes for `SPR_MAX`: badging the reference's own largest
simulated depth as an `estimate` would be worse than the unenforced sentence it replaced. §6's
flagged idiom is for numbers nothing can pin. Both of these could be pinned; nothing had.

### Repairs the memos forced beyond the two

The red team found holes in constants that did *not* come back majority-unanchored, and four of
them were falsified **prose** — a sentence in a header, a gate's own report line, a row in
METHODOLOGY — that measurement contradicts. A falsified claim standing beside a green gate is worse
than no claim, so these are fixed here rather than filed:

- **`payoff-fit.mjs` named the wrong out-of-sample winner.** The flag's third reason read "the CV
  winner is not the held-out winner: **S0 = 8 would have scored better out of sample**". Refitted at
  all fifteen grid points, S0 = 8 scores held-out p95 **16.8416 — worse** than the shipped 12's
  16.7143. The real held-out winner is **S0 = 1.5 at 14.6976**, so the reason survives and survives
  *more strongly* (the gap is 12 → 1.5, not 12 → 8). The sentence is corrected, and the reason is now
  **asserted rather than narrated**: the grid is refitted in `test/payoff-model.test.mjs` and the
  held-out argmin must differ from the selected knob.
- **`MODEL_SE` is 67.6x–74.2x the stub's `se`, not "about 46x".** All three refuters caught it and
  all three were right: 46 is this `se` against §6's 0.1581 pt *tier-EV* se, not against the
  per-pair `se` that `Math.hypot` actually combines it with. Measured over all 15,006 ordered live
  pairs at spr 4, the ratio runs **67.6x to 74.2x**. The only gate was `> 30` — 2.3x looser than the
  claim it existed to protect — so it is now bracketed **from both sides** at the measurement, and
  the header, the fitter and the test all say the same number.
- **The second ridge has a name.** `fitAt` passed a bare `1e-6` to `ols` for *both* latent fits, so
  **twelve of the seventeen** shipped coefficients were conditioned by a literal named in no list,
  no header, no plan row and no brief — while moving it to `RIDGE` takes `potMult`'s `g*x*x`
  coefficient from 5.77 to 9.76. It is now `LATENT_RIDGE`, exported beside `RIDGE` with the reason
  it is larger. **No number entered**: it is the same 1e-6 that was always there, made countable.
- **`RIDGE`'s "fifth decimal" was two orders out.** Measured: between lam 1e-9 and the shipped 1e-7
  the leading `ev` coefficient moves **0.26226887 → 0.26208201** — the fourth significant figure —
  and at lam = 0 it goes to **5.2492** while the selection jumps to S0 = 24. The half that survives
  is the load-bearing half and the docstring now says only that: held-out p95 moves 16.7122 → 16.7143
  across three decades, so the *predictions* are stable even where individual coefficients are not.
- **"CV_FOLDS and CV_SHUFFLES can only move S0, and S0 is already flagged"** is true about scope and
  was false about consequence. Measured: CV_FOLDS 5 → 6 selects **S0 = 8**, 5 → 3 selects 6, and
  CV_SHUFFLES 8 → 3 selects **16** — each turning the suite red, because the frozen block is
  deep-equalled against the fitter's live re-derivation. The sentence now says the scope *and* the
  consequence.
- **"So a typed sizing fails here" is refuted, in four places at once.** Three refuters replaced
  `potLimitLadder`'s derivation with a literal `[3, 9, 27, 81]` behind the same stack cap and passed
  everything — because a derivation and a table that agree are indistinguishable by any check of
  values, which for an *identity* is the correct outcome. The claim is repaired to what the checks
  establish — **a sizing that is not the pot-limit maximum fails**, three independent derivations
  over — in `scripts/gates/solver.mjs` (comment and report line), `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`'s I35 row
  and `docs/V3-PLAN.md` §3.2's measured block.
- **The cap-list clause was self-disabling.** `capListProblems` guarded its tree-derived half with
  `CAPS.omitted.some(/limp/i)`, so deleting "no SB limp" from the declaration *also deleted the
  check written to catch the tree growing an SB limp* — the declaration and the audit of the
  declaration were the same object. The tree is now the authority in both directions: an action the
  tree does not offer must be **declared**, and one it does offer must not be denied. The declared
  set is a parameter so the new direction can be armed, and I35 arms it two more ways. P2 ships no
  on-screen list, so this costs nothing today; it is the clause **P3 leans on**.
- **The stack-off scan reads every module under `scripts/lib`, case-insensitively.** Refuters walked
  past it twice: by renaming the knobs, and by putting form 2 in a **new file**. Renaming is the
  known ceiling of a grep-gate and stays uncaught — the claim being defended is that *form 2's*
  knob was not started, and form 2's knob has names — but "in a different file" had no such excuse.
  The scan is widened, the two evasions are armed, and the sentence that the **quarantine** (not the
  scan) is what carries the claim is now written where the scan is.
- **I35's fabricated drift is derived from the constant it perturbs.** It was a literal
  `{ ...good, epsilonBB: 1e-3 }`, so a refuter loosening `EPSILON_BB` to exactly 1e-3 made the
  "drift" equal the truth and silently collapsed the arming — I35 then failed for a reason unrelated
  to the perturbation. A fabricated violator that can coincide with the truth is not a violator.
- **The parameter count is 15, not 17, and it is now asserted.** `x` is `base - 0.5` and `base` is
  `payoff.mjs`'s projection, so `x` is **identically `dEq/2`** — measured at max deviation 6.9e-17
  over all 300 reference rows — and the `ev` and `q` designs are exactly rank-deficient. Both frozen

  split of one identified quantity (0.6552 for `ev`, 3.2731 for `q`). Nothing here is wrong and no

  The collinearity is asserted so the count cannot quietly become true or quietly get worse; the
  *repair* the memos propose — dropping `x*g` from both designs — would change the frozen block, and
  re-freezing a measured artifact to improve its description is the §0.4 ceremony, not a tidy-up.

### Findings recorded, not acted on here

Real, named so they cannot be lost, and not filed as blockers:

1. **The three solver thresholds are anchored as interval membership, not to their values.**
   Measured live bands, with the shipped value inside each: `EPSILON_BB` roughly **[8e-6, 1.5e-3]**
   (≈195x wide), `ITER_CAP` bounded below near **700** and not at all above, `TWO_SEED_TOL_POT`
   roughly **(6e-6, 5.05e-2)** (≈8,400x wide). Every one of those bounds is a real, two-sided,
   written-to-fail measurement — ε's upper edge is the payoff's own `se`, read live, so a quieter
   payoff really does force it down — but inside the band only a literal `assert.equal` fires, and a
   literal pin fires just as loudly on an improvement. This is a fair description of a threshold and
   it should be the description, not the implication that the gates pick the number out.
2. **`ITER_CAP`'s quoted margin is ~3x, not 4x.** S-A's crossing at iteration 456, and this lane's
   re-measured 332 / 316 on the checkdown stub, are **seed-0** readings; the gate solves three seeds
   and the worst does not clear ε until roughly 662 (T100) / 650 (T40). The anchor text should quote
   the worst-seed crossing, because that is the number the gate depends on.
3. **`TWO_SEED_TOL_POT`'s "~4x the measurement" is about an axis that is not live.** The 0.035%
   figure is S-A's *payoff*-axis spread, and the payoff axis is inert under a checkdown source —
   which I35 checks rather than assumes. Against the axis actually exercised (init), measured at
   0.0004% / 0.0006% of pot, the gate sits at roughly **250x** the measurement.
4. **`mirrorBound`'s multiplier is the one acknowledged choice, and its floor is higher than stated.**
   Tightening fails at ~0.4 against I35's own residual (1.42e-14 bb), but `test/cfr.test.mjs`'s
   conserving position-aware fixture reaches **2.84e-14**, so the binding measurement is that one and
   the margin at 8 is about **10x**, not 20x. Upward it is free by roughly 1e8. It can only move the
   sensitivity of a residual check, never a shipped number.
5. **`simplexBound`'s clause (b) has no fabricated violator, and its `N` is never exercised.** The
   bound is tight — at `N*EPSILON/4` five of six solves breach it, one ulp of headroom — but
   loosening by 1e6 is green, and both I35 and `test/cfr.test.mjs` only ever call `simplexBound(3)`,
   so the linear-in-`N` form is an argument rather than a measurement. Six lines of arming would
   close the loosening direction.
6. **§6's "rendered by the Method view" leg is unmet for every solver constant, and honestly so.**
   `solver.epsilonBB`, `solver.iterCap`, `solver.twoSeedTolPot` and `solver.sizingLadder` live in
   `cfr.mjs`'s `CONSTANTS` and reach neither `data/model.json` nor `index.html`; I35 reports
   "0 shipped constants blocks to check" with the detector armed, deferred to P3. No shipped
   statement fails when one of these moves, because there is no shipped statement.
7. **The single-raise-size abstraction is flagged one notch weaker than §6 asked.** Offering exactly
   one raise size per node — the pot-limit maximum — where a real pot-limit game offers a continuum
   from the min-raise to the pot is an abstraction choice, and it appears in `CAPS.modelled` (a
   feature) rather than `CAPS.omitted` (an omission). The ladder's *values* need no flag; this
   choice still does.
8. **`FIT` is a standing tripwire on `data/model.json`.** `rowsFor` reads the shipped model's
   per-cell features, so any regeneration of the model moves all seventeen coefficients and fails the
   re-derivation test. That is correct behaviour and a genuine tripwire — but P3 regenerates the
   model to emit `data/equilibrium.json`, and whoever does it should know the estimator's frozen
   block is downstream of that decision before they hit it.
