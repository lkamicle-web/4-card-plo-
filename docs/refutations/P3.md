# P3 red team — the refutation record

Three independent refuters were pointed at every new opinion constant this phase shipped — the
equilibrium baseline's quantization step, the four byte budgets the dual build and the payload
forced, D6's reserved raise, the checkdown matrix's board budget and the chance measure the solve
is taken under — with one instruction: **falsify it**. Perturb the number, delete the flag, replace
the anchor's prose with a lie, and report what the repository does about it. Eight constants (or
constant-shaped claims), spread over sixteen tally keys, drew **thirty memos**. This file is the
record, committed with the phase.

**Why it is a file and not a paragraph.** The house rule is *constants need anchors*: a number
either stands on a measurement, or it ships flagged, gated and badged with the reason it cannot.
That rule is only worth what the audit behind it is worth, and an audit that leaves no trace is a
claim rather than a check. The memos below are reproduced **verbatim**, including the places where
refuters disagree with each other, the places where a memo is wrong about the repository, and every
sentence critical of work this phase had just finished. Nothing was trimmed to look better. Two
memos say out loud that the tally row they were handed no longer describes the code (the board
budget's "12.5k–25k band", which decision 13 replaced with 400,000); both are printed as written,
because a red team that is quietly corrected before publication is a red team nobody can audit.

## The verdict

A constant is **majority-unanchored** when more than half the refuters who attacked it returned
*unanchorable*. **One** claim came back that way — `baselineQuant` — across three of the sixteen
tally keys (the same constant described three ways: by role, by its full §6 row, and by name).
Every other constant drew *falsifiable* from every refuter who touched it.

## What changed because of it

### 1. `baselineQuant` — the one majority-unanchored constant

**What was refuted, precisely.** Not the table. All six refuters re-derived it exactly
(`0.05 → 4,589 B / 15 MIX`, `0.01 → 4,964 B / 20 MIX`, `0.001 → 5,357 B / 23 MIX`) and said so;
one called it "documentation that happens to be executable". What was refuted is the claim that the
table **anchors the value**:

* nothing in GREEN ever ran it. `0.02` — a step the table does not price — regenerates honestly and
  ships 55/55 gates, 591/591 tests, 2/2 variants current. So do `0.05` and `0.5`.
* the first bound that bites is D6's 12 KB sub-budget at `q ≤ 1e-5`, five orders of magnitude away
  and a bound on **bytes**, not on the resolution the anchor argues about.
* the anchor's own prose ships into both artifacts and renders beside the value
  (`Tiers are quantized to <q> — <anchor>`), so at `0.02` the page printed a table pricing three
  steps, none of them the one it was attached to. One refuter replaced the table with fabricated
  figures, regenerated, rebuilt, and shipped it green.
* the selection argument itself — *which mixes a tier-level surface can paint* — is a judgment. It
  is a good one, and it is not a measurement.

**Disposition — no replacement anchor was invented.** V3-PLAN §6's rule when an anchor is refuted is
the flagged idiom, and that is what the constant now ships as:

| leg | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| named in `constants` | `baselineQuant.kind: 'anchored'` | `kind: 'estimate'`, with `flag` beside `anchor`, in `data/equilibrium.json` **and** in `model.baselineTiers.quantFlag`, so lite carries it too |
| labelled in the Method view | the anchor rendered as prose beside the value | the value renders with the Method view's `estimate` badge (`UNANCHORED['baselineQuant']`) and the flag's sentence under it |
| bounded by a gate | D6's 12 KB sub-budget — a 500× band | **I36 clause (e)**, below |

**I36(e), and why it is not a re-anchoring.** The clause re-derives the anchor table **from the
shipped payload's own full-precision strategies** at every verify — no solve, single-digit
milliseconds — and asserts three things:

1. the shipped step is one of the steps the shipped anchor **prices**;
2. every figure the anchor quotes (`B`, `MIX`) is the **measured** figure for that step;
3. the shipped shared-core block is **exactly** the quantization of the shipped strategies at the
   shipped step — the two artifacts are one artifact.

That kills the 500× band, the unpriced step and the fabricable table. It does **not** make 0.01 the
only shippable value: `0.05` and `0.001` are priced rows and would still pass. That residue is the
whole content of the flag, and it is said out loud rather than papered over — which is the
difference between flagging a constant and re-anchoring it.

### 2. `VARIANTS.full.budgets.total` — the anchor was stale, and the repair is the honest direction

Two refuters, independently, measured `index-full.html` at **628,036 B (613.3 KB)** against the
`618,127 B (603.6 KB)` its `budgetSource` claims, and one reconciled the 9,909 B gap to the byte
against METHODOLOGY §9.11's own measurement of the vs-GTO block (`618,127 + 10,035 − 126 = 628,036`).
So `app` was re-measured when the mode landed and full's `total` was not; D9 printed both numbers in
one sentence every run and nothing compared them.

The repair does **not** raise the budget. A fresh measured+5% would be 644 KB; 634 KB is kept, and
the prose now says what is true — the ceiling is held **below** measured+5%, the live measurement is
628,036 B, and the real headroom is 3.4%. Being tighter than the rule is the conservative direction;
printing a measurement that no longer describes the artifact is not.

### 3. Recorded, not repaired here — with reasons

* **The chance measure's disclosure is self-referential.** All three refuters deleted
  `no card removal between the two players — chance is the product of marginals` from `CAPS.omitted`,
  regenerated and rebuilt, and shipped an undisclosed abstraction with 55/55 gates, 591/591 tests and
  2/2 builds current. `capListProblems` ties only the SB limp and the sixth raise to the actual tree;
  every other omission is checked against a copy of itself. The abstraction's **cost** is gated hard
  and independently confirmed (I33(h) fails by name on all 43 undealable pairs when the accessor is
  forced to answer `supported:true`; the 43 and the 3.58e-5 mass are recomputed from the artifact
  every run; I35 bounds the exact-zero-sum property the choice buys). It is the **flag** that has no
  teeth. The remedy the memos converge on — a required-omissions floor, a short list of abstraction
  flags that must be present in `CAPS.omitted` — is a change to I35's cap-list clause rather than to
  anything this stage owns, and it is on the record here for the milestone close.
* **`BOARDS` is not asserted against S-A's regime.** `test/checkdown-matrix.test.mjs:94` pins
  `BOARDS === 400000` literally and I35(c) fails at 25,000, so both directions are covered; what is
  not asserted is `BOARDS === SA_REPRO.boards`, so a later phase could move to 1,000,000 and quietly
  stop being in the regime `twoSeedTolPot`'s "~4× margin" was measured in. Recorded.
* **The two seed names have no falsifier.** Re-rolling `checkdown-C`/`checkdown-D` and regenerating
  both 400k matrices ships green. At 400k's 2.3× margin the incentive to seed-pick is small; the
  mechanism that would stop one at a noisier count is absent. Recorded.
* **I35(c)'s failure message templates the live board count** into "at the ${BOARDS} boards the
  anchor itself was measured at", so a failing 25,000-board run prints a sentence that is true only
  at 400,000. Cosmetic, on the failure path only. Recorded.
* **Two documentation errors in `equilibrium.mjs`**, both found by the same refuter and both fixed in
  this pass: the block's JSDoc said "90.9% of its named sub-budget" where the live reading was 91.4%,
  and `sourceHash`'s comment said "D9 recomputes it every run" when only `test/equilibrium.test.mjs`
  does.

**No blockers.** The one majority-unanchored constant took §6's flagged idiom cleanly — it has a
`constants` entry, a Method-view rendering and a bounding gate — so nothing here stops the milestone.
No tolerance was widened anywhere in this pass, and every clause added was shown to fail before it
was believed.
