# V4 red team — the refutation record

Three independent refuters were pointed at every constant and constant-shaped claim the 9-max seat
ladder shipped — the early-seat rules and the numbers they derive, the anchor sentence and the
estimate flag beside them, the ring artifact's seeds, tolerances, wall budget and ceiling factor,
the census thresholds, the `LJ` naming, every byte ceiling §2.7 raised, and the two frozen skill
records — with one instruction: **falsify it**. Perturb the number, delete the flag, replace the
anchor's prose with a lie, and report what the repository does about it. Thirty-six tally keys drew
**one hundred and eight memos**. This file is the record, committed with the run.

**Why it is a file and not a paragraph.** The house rule is *constants need anchors*: a number
either stands on a measurement, or it ships flagged, gated and badged with the reason it cannot.
That rule is only worth what the audit behind it is worth, and an audit that leaves no trace is a
claim rather than a check. The memos below are reproduced **verbatim**, as their refuters filed
them, including the places where they disagree with each other, the places where a memo is wrong
about the repository, and every sentence critical of work this run had just finished. Nothing was
trimmed to look better. One memo describes `LEGACY_PIN = 655` at `gates/ring.mjs:574` — a number
that survives only inside the comment above the live pin of 487 — and says so itself; two others
correct it. All three are printed as written, because a red team that is quietly corrected before
publication is a red team nobody can audit.

## What changed because of it

Six dispositions, nine gate clauses, four documentation repairs, and a set of recorded findings
that changed no code. **Every clause was armed before it was believed**: the perturbation that
shipped green in a refuter's memo was re-run against the new clause and made to fail
(`test/gates-ring.test.mjs`, `test/ring-gate.test.mjs`).

### 1. `baseRRule` — the one anchor that was refuted outright, and the honest disposition

**What was refuted.** Not the choice. `flat` still ships, for the reason R1 gives. What was refuted
is that a measurement chose it. §2.3 predicted R1 "moves painted width in **8 of the 12 lanes**"
through `depthWidthFactor`; measured, it moves painted width in **0 of the 12**, because
`TF3.DEFAULT_LANE` sets `depthWidth: false` on every fixture lane and `depthWidthFactor` therefore
returns exactly 1 and never reads `baseR`. On the score surface `baseR` is one per-seat common
factor over a whole (pos, node, v) table, and a common factor cannot re-rank a percentile cut. All
three refuters shipped `'step'` and got verify 69/69, the whole test suite, `build --check` current
and I49's **3,214,728** nine-seat tiers **byte-identical**.

**Disposition — no replacement anchor was invented.** The constant now ships the way §6 says an
unanchorable one must:

| leg | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| gated | I51(a) admits `'flat'` or `'step'` and nothing else | unchanged — and that is the whole of the bound, said out loud |
| flagged in `constants` | `ladder.flag` named `baseRaise`/`baseR` only | names **`baseRRule`** too, and I51(a) asserts that it does |
| labelled in the Method view | `LD.baseRRule` already rendered | unchanged, and I51(a) now asserts the rendering |
| badged | not badged | `UNANCHORED['ladder.baseRRule']`, asserted by I51(a)'s badge-map clause |

The rejected alternative's readings are recorded as **identical on every gated surface** rather than
as "also passing", and §2.3 and §3's R1 carry the falsification in the plan's own annotation idiom.
The one surface where the choice is live — `depthWidth: true`, +2.3 % of painted width at UTG at
d = 40 — is named as ungated rather than quietly gated by widening something.

## 1. `constants.ladder.earlyStep = 0.77 - a SECOND constant with the same value as straddle.seat, which keeps its own value and gains derivedFrom:'ladder.earlyStep' rather than becoming a live reference (anchor: 1/sqrt(1.250*1.350), the geometric mean of baseRaise's UTG->HJ and HJ->CO steps) - is one step of the SIX-seat ladder the right step for three seats that ladder never contained, and does the two-constants-one-anchor split buy anything beyond keeping I26 and I51(a) from fighting?`

> **Refuters:** 3 · **called unanchorable:** 0 / 3 · **majority verdict: falsifiable** · **disposition: anchored — the anchor arithmetic reproduces and is now re-derived from the live table by I51(a); §10 q2 answered in its favour**


**Refuter 1.**

**Attack.** (a) `earlyStep` 0.77 → 0.80 alone. (b) `earlyStep` *and* `straddle.seat` → 0.80
together, so the equality survives. (c) The anchor's own arithmetic and its extension, read against
the three steps `baseRaise` actually contains.

**Result.** (a) fails **I51(a)** (`straddle.seat 0.77 !== ladder.earlyStep 0.8`) and **I49**
(4,018 of 26,136 nine-seat settings move, 19,218 cell tiers) — the wiring is live and the constant is
material: a 3.9 % change in the step moves 15.4 % of the frozen nine-seat surface. (b) is the
interesting one: with both moved, **I51 passes, I26 passes**, and only the *six-seat* legacy fixture
I32 and I49 fail. So what actually pins 0.77 is the frozen six-seat fixture, not I26 — I26's
straddle-direction claim is qualitative and 0.80 satisfies it. (c) the anchor arithmetic checks out
(`1/√(1.250·1.350)` = 0.7698 → 0.77, and `baseRaise` really does step 1.2500 / 1.3500 / 1.6667). The
extension does not. The model's three measured steps **grow toward the button**, so extrapolating
*backward* should use a step no larger than the nearest one, 1.2500 (`earlyStep` 0.80); the shipped
rule instead uses 1.2987, a step **steeper than any adjacent-seat step at the front of the ladder**,
because it averages the nearest step with the more distant HJ→CO one. Extending the ladder's own
second-order trend (the step multiplier itself, ln-linear through 1.25/1.35/1.667) predicts the front
of the ladder *flattens* and then inverts — a shape I51(a1)'s strict-increase clause would reject.
So "a geometric ladder is the only anchor the model has" is not right: the model's evidence is three
*unequal* steps, and holding the step constant is itself an unanchored choice, made in the direction
the evidence points away from.

**Verdict.** Falsifiable (I51(a) equality + I49), not unanchorable. The two-constants split buys
exactly what §2.3 says and no more: I26 and I51(a) do not fight, and I51(a) pins them equal. What it
does not buy is any evidence for the number at the three seats the ladder never contained — that
